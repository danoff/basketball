# NBA Draft Combine Rookie Defensive Model — Results Writeup

**Version:** MP ≥ 100 final no-shuttle model  
**Timestamp:** 20260517_123051_CT  
**Outcome:** Rookie-season Defensive Box Plus/Minus (`DBPM`)  
**Training years:** 2023, 2024, and 2025 NBA Draft Combine classes  
**Rookie outcome seasons:** 2023-24, 2024-25, and 2025-26  

---

## 1. Research Question

This analysis asks whether selected NBA Draft Combine measurements are associated with rookie-season defensive impact, measured by **Defensive Box Plus/Minus (`DBPM`)**.

The model is intentionally limited to pre-draft combine variables. It does **not** include college statistics, draft position, NBA team context, position labels, or subjective scouting grades.

The goal is not to build a complete player projection system. The goal is to test whether a simple, interpretable combine-only model can provide a useful defensive screening signal.

---

## 2. Data and Sample

The modeling dataset combines prior-year NBA Draft Combine measurements with rookie-season NBA outcomes.

| Draft Class | Rookie Outcome Season |
|---|---|
| 2023 | 2023-24 |
| 2024 | 2024-25 |
| 2025 | 2025-26 |

The training data initially included players with rookie DBPM outcomes and the required combine measurements. A further restriction was applied:

> Players were included in the final model only if they played **at least 100 NBA minutes** as rookies.

This restriction was added because very small NBA minute samples can produce unstable DBPM values. For example, a player with only a few minutes may have an extreme DBPM that reflects noise rather than meaningful defensive performance.

### Final sample

| Sample | n |
|---|---:|
| Unrestricted final no-shuttle model, MP > 0 | 108 |
| Restricted final no-shuttle model, MP ≥ 100 | 89 |

The final reported model uses the **MP ≥ 100** sample.

---

## 3. Dependent Variable

The dependent variable is:

```text
DBPM
```

`DBPM` stands for **Defensive Box Plus/Minus**. It is a Basketball-Reference estimate of a player's defensive contribution per 100 possessions relative to league average, derived from box-score statistics and team context.

Because rookie DBPM can be noisy and context-dependent, the results should be interpreted as exploratory.

---

## 4. Final Model Specification

The final model excludes `Shuttle_Run`, because prior testing showed that removing it did not reduce explanatory power and improved model parsimony.

The final model is:

```text
DBPM ~ Hand_Width + Standing_Vertical_Leap + Lane_Agility_Time + Wingspan_in + Weight_lbs
```

In equation form:

\[
DBPM = \beta_0
+ \beta_1(HandWidth)
+ \beta_2(StandingVertical)
+ \beta_3(LaneAgility)
+ \beta_4(Wingspan)
+ \beta_5(Weight)
+ \epsilon
\]

---

## 5. Model Fit

### Fit comparison

| Model | Minute Restriction | n | R² | Adjusted R² | AIC | RMSE |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Unrestricted final no-shuttle model | MP > 0 | 108 | 0.182 | 0.142 | 390.974 | 1.399 |
| Restricted final no-shuttle model | MP ≥ 100 | 89 | 0.233 | 0.187 | 246.773 | 0.905 |

### Fit interpretation

The **MP ≥ 100** model explains approximately **23.3%** of the variation in rookie DBPM.

After adjusting for the number of predictors, the model explains approximately **18.7%** of the variation.

The model's RMSE is **0.905 DBPM points**, meaning that the typical prediction error is a little under one DBPM point in the restricted training sample.

The 100-minute restriction improved model performance substantially:

- R² increased from **0.182** to **0.233**
- Adjusted R² increased from **0.142** to **0.187**
- RMSE decreased from **1.399** to **0.905**

This suggests that excluding very low-minute rookie seasons reduces noise in the dependent variable.

## 7. Predictor Interpretations

### 7.1 Weight

```text
Coefficient = 0.022
p = 0.003
```

`Weight_lbs` is the strongest positive predictor in the final model.

Holding the other variables constant, each additional pound is associated with approximately:

```text
+0.022 DBPM
```

A 10-pound difference corresponds to approximately:

```text
10 × 0.022 = +0.22 DBPM
```

This coefficient is statistically significant at the 1% level.

Basketball interpretation:

> Rookie defensive translation may benefit from functional physicality. Heavier players may be better able to hold position, absorb contact, rebound, screen through contact, contest physically, and survive early NBA strength demands.

This should not be interpreted as “heavier is always better.” The coefficient is conditional on the other variables in the model.

---

### 7.2 Hand Width

```text
Coefficient = 0.390
p = 0.037
```

`Hand_Width` is also a statistically significant positive predictor.

Holding other variables constant, each additional inch of hand width is associated with approximately:

```text
+0.390 DBPM
```

This result is significant at the 5% level.

Basketball interpretation:

> Wider hands may relate to ball disruption, deflections, grip strength, rebounding control, steals, and finishing defensive possessions.

Among the combine-only variables tested, hand width became one of the clearest positive signals after restricting the sample to rookies with at least 100 minutes.

---

### 7.3 Standing Vertical Leap

```text
Coefficient = 0.046
p = 0.256
```

`Standing_Vertical_Leap` has a positive coefficient but is not statistically significant in the MP ≥ 100 model.

Earlier unrestricted models showed a stronger standing vertical signal, but after applying the 100-minute threshold, the coefficient became smaller and less precise.

Basketball interpretation:

> Standing vertical leap remains directionally plausible as a measure of quick-twitch pop, contest ability, second-jump potential, and vertical explosiveness, but it is not a reliable independent predictor in the final restricted model.

---

### 7.4 Lane Agility Time

```text
Coefficient = 0.291
p = 0.328
```

`Lane_Agility_Time` is not statistically significant.

The coefficient is positive, which is counterintuitive because higher lane agility time means slower performance.

This should **not** be interpreted as evidence that slower lane agility improves defense. More likely explanations include:

- noise in the lane agility measure,
- overlap with body type,
- role differences,
- limited sample size,
- or the possibility that lane agility is less informative than other combine variables for rookie DBPM.

Public-facing interpretation:

> Lane agility was retained as a standard combine movement measure, but it did not provide a clear independent signal in the final model.

---

### 7.5 Wingspan

```text
Coefficient = -0.084
p = 0.032
```

`Wingspan_in` is statistically significant but negative.

This is counterintuitive, because longer wingspan is usually considered beneficial for defense.

This result should be interpreted cautiously. It likely reflects multicollinearity and body-type composition rather than evidence that wingspan is bad for defense. Wingspan overlaps with other physical measurements, and once weight, hand width, vertical leap, and lane agility are included, its independent coefficient becomes difficult to interpret.

Possible explanations:

- wingspan overlaps with height and standing reach,
- some long players in the sample may be slower-developing bigs,
- stronger/heavier players may capture the physicality signal better,
- the model does not include position or role,
- and rookie DBPM is sensitive to team context and playing time.

Recommended interpretation:

> Wingspan should not be framed as harmful. In this specification, wingspan does not provide a clean positive independent signal after accounting for weight, hand width, vertical leap, and lane agility.

---

## 8. Why Shuttle Run Was Removed

An earlier model included `Shuttle_Run`:

```text
DBPM ~ Hand_Width + Standing_Vertical_Leap + Shuttle_Run + Lane_Agility_Time + Wingspan_in + Weight_lbs
```

That model was compared to the no-shuttle version on the same 108-player unrestricted subset.

| Model | n | R² | Adjusted R² | AIC | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| With Shuttle Run | 108 | 0.182 | 0.133 | 392.955 | 1.399 |
| Without Shuttle Run | 108 | 0.182 | 0.142 | 390.974 | 1.399 |

Removing `Shuttle_Run`:

- preserved R²,
- improved adjusted R²,
- improved AIC,
- and did not change RMSE.

Therefore, `Shuttle_Run` was removed for parsimony.

---

## 9. Main Statistical Finding

The final MP ≥ 100 model suggests that rookie DBPM is modestly predictable from NBA Draft Combine measurements.

The strongest positive signals are:

1. **Weight**
2. **Hand width**

The weaker or unclear signals are:

1. **Standing vertical leap** — positive but not significant
2. **Lane agility time** — not significant and counterintuitive sign
3. **Wingspan** — statistically significant but counterintuitive negative sign, likely reflecting multicollinearity or role/body-type composition

A concise statistical takeaway:

> After restricting the training sample to rookies who played at least 100 NBA minutes, the model explains about 23% of rookie DBPM variation. Weight and hand width emerge as the clearest positive combine signals.

---

## 10. Basketball Interpretation

The model's interpretation shifted over the course of development.

Early single-year models suggested that shuttle run and standing reach might be useful. But after expanding the training sample to 2023–2025 and applying a 100-minute rookie threshold, the clearest signals became:

- physical mass,
- hand width,
- and, more weakly, vertical explosiveness.

The current basketball interpretation is:

> Early NBA defensive impact appears more associated with functional physicality and ball-disruption traits than with raw length alone.

In practical scouting language:

> The model tends to reward players who have enough body mass to handle NBA contact and wide enough hands to plausibly contribute to disruption, rebounding control, and defensive possession-finishing.

## 11. Suggested Formal Results Paragraph

A multiple linear regression was estimated to examine whether NBA Draft Combine measurements predicted rookie-season Defensive Box Plus/Minus (DBPM). The sample included players from the 2023, 2024, and 2025 draft classes who recorded complete data for hand width, standing vertical leap, lane agility time, wingspan, weight, and rookie DBPM. The final model was restricted to players who logged at least 100 NBA minutes as rookies, producing a sample of 89 players. The model explained 23.3% of the variance in rookie DBPM, \(R^2 = .233\), adjusted \(R^2 = .187\), with an RMSE of 0.905. Weight was a statistically significant positive predictor of DBPM, \(b = 0.022\), \(p = .003\), as was hand width, \(b = 0.390\), \(p = .037\). Standing vertical leap was positive but not significant, \(b = 0.046\), \(p = .256\). Lane agility time was not significant, \(b = 0.291\), \(p = .328\). Wingspan was statistically significant but negative, \(b = -0.084\), \(p = .032\), a result that should be interpreted cautiously because of overlap among body-size variables and the absence of role or position controls. Overall, the model provides exploratory evidence that rookie defensive impact is modestly associated with functional physicality, especially weight and hand width, while pure length and agility measures are less stable independent predictors.

---

## 12. Public-Facing Summary

> Using 2023–2025 rookie-linked NBA Draft Combine data and restricting the training sample to players with at least 100 rookie NBA minutes, the model explains about 23% of rookie DBPM variation. The clearest positive signals are weight and hand width. This suggests that early NBA defensive translation may depend more on functional physicality and ball-disruption traits than on length alone. The model remains exploratory and should be used as a screening tool, not a scouting grade.

---

## 13. Caveats

This model should remain clearly labeled as exploratory.

| Caveat | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Rookie DBPM is noisy | It depends on role, scheme, teammates, and minutes. |
| MP ≥ 100 helps but does not solve context | Players still have different roles and opportunities. |
| Combine participation is selective | Some prospects skip events or lack complete data. |
| Sample size is modest | 89 rows is useful but still limited. |
| Multicollinearity is likely | Size variables overlap with each other. |
| Wingspan coefficient is counterintuitive | This should not be overinterpreted as a negative basketball trait. |
| Position is not included | This improves simplicity but hides role differences. |
| College production is not included | The model remains combine-only by design. |
| Not causal | The regression identifies associations, not proof of defensive ability. |
